Hidden Latin Roots

Understanding French vocabulary through etymology
Passer en Français 🇫🇷

Welcome! Many French words look similar because they share the same Latin ancestors. By learning to spot these roots, you can guess the meaning of hundreds of complex words. Let's discover 4 magical families.

1. -SPECT- / -SPIC- From Latin specere / spectare: to look, to observe

All these words contain the idea of vision, looking, or observing, modified by a prefix.

Aspect
ad- (toward) + spect
The way something presents itself to the eye; its appearance.
Circumspect
circum- (around) + spect
Someone who looks all around before acting; cautious, wary.
Inspect
in- (into) + spect
To look closely inside or examine something very carefully.
Prospect
pro- (forward) + spect
To look forward, to explore an area or market for resources/clients.
Respect
re- (back / again) + spect
To look back at, to stop and show consideration or esteem for someone.
Spectacle
spectare + -culum (means)
What is presented to the eyes; a public show or sight.
Suspect
sub- (under) + spect
To look from underneath; to view someone or something with mistrust.
Retrospective + Bonus!
retro- (backward) + spect
A look back at a sequence of past events or an artist's life work.
Perspicacious + Bonus!
per- (through) + spic- (see)
Someone who sees clearly through things; sharp-witted, insightful.
2. -JECT- From Latin jacere / jactare: to throw, to cast

All these words share the physical or figurative action of throwing or pushing something.

Abject
ab- (away from) + ject
Thrown far away, cast off by society; despicable, completely low.
Bijection
bi- (two) + ject
Math term: a matching where elements of two sets are "thrown together" one-to-one.
Dejection
de- (down) + ject
Excrement or waste matter that is cast downward out of the body.
Eject
ex- (out of) + ject
To throw out, to expel or force out with speed.
Inject
in- (into) + ject
To throw or force a fluid inside a body or space.
Object
ob- (against) + ject
To throw an argument against an idea; to oppose or voice disagreement.
Subjective
sub- (under) + ject
Depending on the subject (what is cast underneath, built on one's own perception).
Trajectory + Bonus!
trans- (across) + ject
The path followed by a flying object thrown across space.
Projector + Bonus!
pro- (forward) + ject
A device that throws light forward onto a surface.
3. The "-ECT" Trap 4 different verbs that look identical today!

Watch out! These words all end in -ect- or -ection, but they actually stem from 4 completely distinct Latin verbs:

Group A: From Latin Regere (To lead straight, rule → past participle: rectus)
Correction
cum- (with) + rect
The act of straightening out or making something "right" together.
Direction
di- / de- (toward) + rect
The act of guiding in a straight line toward a goal.
Erection
ex- (out of) + rect
The act of raising or building something straight up.
Group B: From Latin Legere (To gather, choose, read → past participle: lectus)
Election
ex- (out of) + lect
The act of choosing or picking someone out of a group.
Selection + Bonus!
se- (apart) + lect
To set apart by choosing the best elements; sorting.
Group C: From Latin Tegere (To cover, protect → past participle: tectus)
Detect
de- (remove) + tect
To remove the cover or roof; to uncover what was hidden.
Protection
pro- (before) + tect
Placing a cover or shield in front of something to defend it.
Group D: From Latin Nectere (To bind, tie)
Connect
cum- (together) + nectere
To bind, link, or tie multiple things together.
4. -SECT- From Latin secare: to cut

This root looks like -spect but has an entirely sharp, cutting meaning!

Section
secare + -tion
A cut, or a distinct part that has been separated/cut from a whole.
Insect + Bonus!
in- (into) + sect
A tiny animal whose body is "cut into" three distinct segments.
Dissection + Bonus!
dis- (apart) + sect
The act of cutting an organism apart piece by piece to study it.
💡 Tip for English Speakers:

English borrowed tons of vocabulary from French and Latin. Almost all these words have exact cognates in English (aspect, inspect, reject, selection, protect...). Mastering the root unlocks advanced vocabulary in BOTH languages seamlessly!
